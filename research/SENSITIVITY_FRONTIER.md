# Exact sensitivity at a fixed force budget

The [exact robustness cost](CLAIMS.md#c1-exact-attainable-angle-cost) is the
budget at which the smallest achievable angle slope first reaches zero. The
same finite-space model also determines the entire budget-dependent minimum.
Optimizing the supporting multiplier, instead of fixing it at the robust-cost
optimizer, turns the earlier lower certificate into an attainable equality.

## Statement and conventions

Keep the model, force normalization, and nonzero unwrapped target of
[the claims page](CLAIMS.md). Assume the nominally closed complex force space
$V$ is nonzero, with Hermitian phase matrix $K$ and positive matrix $D$.
Write

$$
t=|\Theta_0|>0,\qquad \lambda=\|K\|,\qquad
\rho(\zeta)=\|K-\zeta D\|,\qquad r=\min_{\zeta\in\mathbb R}\rho(\zeta).
$$

If $K=0$, the target is infeasible at every budget. Otherwise, for a finite
budget $B\geq t/\lambda$, define

$$
s_V(B;t)=\min\left\{|\chi|:\Theta=\Theta_0,\ E\leq B,\ f_1,f_2\in V\right\}.
$$

Then the minimum is attained and

$$
\boxed{\quad
s_V(B;t)=\max\left\{0,\ \sup_{\zeta\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}}
\frac{t-B\rho(\zeta)}{|\zeta|}\right\}.
\quad} \tag{1}
$$

The same value and an attaining pair exist if the budget condition is
$E=B$. Budgets below $t/\lambda$ are infeasible; formula (1) is not assigned
a sensitivity value there. At the nominal endpoint the displayed supremum
need not be attained by a nonzero multiplier. This does not obstruct
attainment by forces.

The function is nonincreasing and convex in its feasible budget variable.
It vanishes exactly when $Br\geq t$. In particular, if $r=0$, no finite
budget eliminates the slope. For $r>0$, its first zero is the existing
angle-robust threshold $t/r$.

## Joint numerical range and exact duality

It suffices to take $\Theta_0=t$: reversing one force changes the signs of
both $\Theta$ and $\chi$, while preserving cost. Set

$$
x=\frac{a+b}{\sqrt2},\qquad y=\frac{a-b}{\sqrt2},\qquad
w=\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix},\qquad
M=\begin{pmatrix}K&0\\0&-K\end{pmatrix},\qquad
N=\begin{pmatrix}D&0\\0&-D\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Then $\Theta=w^\dagger Mw$, $q:=-\chi=w^\dagger Nw$, and
$E=\|w\|^2$. Consider the unit-cost attainable set

$$
C=\{(w^\dagger Mw,w^\dagger Nw):\|w\|=1\}.
$$

This is the numerical range of $M+iN$, identified with a subset of
$\mathbb R^2$. It is compact and convex by the established
Toeplitz–Hausdorff theorem [S17, Theorem 6.1]. Swapping $x$ and $y$ makes it
centrally symmetric. In particular $0\in C$; directly, choose $x=y$ with
$\|x\|^2=1/2$. Its support function is

$$
h_C(\alpha,\beta)
=\lambda_{\max}(\alpha M+\beta N)
=\|\alpha K+\beta D\|. \tag{2}
$$

Consequently the attainable set under $E=B$ is $BC$. The set under
$E\leq B$ is the same, because $C$ is convex and contains the origin.
Every point here is generated by a single vector $w$, hence by two
simultaneous coherent forces. Convexity introduces no randomization,
concatenation, or average over gate protocols.

There is also a direct check of the budget equality. For a nonzero target,
both $a$ and $b$ are nonzero. Replacing them by $ca,c^{-1}b$, with $c>0$,
preserves $\Theta$ and $\chi$. Its cost
$c^2\|a\|^2+c^{-2}\|b\|^2$ takes every value at least as large as the
original cost along a suitable continuous branch. Thus an unused budget
can always be spent without changing either observable in this resource
model.

Fix a proposed slope cap $s\geq0$ and the vertical segment
$S_s=\{(t,q):|q|\leq s\}$. Exact feasibility is $BC\cap S_s\ne\varnothing$.
The support inequalities (2) show that feasibility requires

$$
t\leq B\rho(\zeta)+|\zeta|s
\qquad\text{for every real }\zeta. \tag{3}
$$

They are also sufficient. If the two compact convex sets were disjoint,
strict separation would give real $\alpha,\beta$ such that

$$
\alpha t-|\beta|s>B\|\alpha K+\beta D\|.
$$

The right side is nonnegative, so $\alpha>0$. Dividing by $\alpha$ and
putting $\zeta=-\beta/\alpha$ contradicts (3). The case $\beta=0$ would
contradict nominal feasibility $t\leq B\lambda$. Rearranging (3) proves
(1), including its endpoint convention. Compactness gives primal
attainment independently of whether a multiplier attains its supremum.

The nonincreasing convex dependence follows because (1) is a supremum of
affine decreasing functions and zero. Its zero condition follows from
(3) with $s=0$ and $\min_\zeta\rho(\zeta)=r$.

## Constructing attaining forces

### A positive minimum above the nominal budget

Suppose $B>t/\lambda$ and $s_V(B;t)>0$. The ratio in (1) tends to
$-\infty$ as $\zeta\to0$, while its limit as $|\zeta|\to\infty$ is
$-B\|D\|<0$. Therefore a finite nonzero maximizing $\widehat\zeta$ exists.
Let $\widehat\rho=\rho(\widehat\zeta)$ and

$$
F=\ker(M-\widehat\zeta N-\widehat\rho I).
$$

At any optimal point, equality in (3) forces

$$
q=\operatorname{sgn}(\widehat\zeta)s_V(B;t),\qquad
t-\widehat\zeta q=B\widehat\rho.
$$

Its normalized vector must lie in $F$. Hence $t/B$ lies between the
smallest and largest eigenvalues, $\mu_-$ and $\mu_+$, of the compression
$M_F=P_FM|_F$.

If $\mu_-<\mu_+$, choose orthonormal eigenvectors $v_-,v_+$ at those
endpoints, and put

$$
p=\frac{t/B-\mu_-}{\mu_+-\mu_-},\qquad
v=\sqrt{1-p}\,v_-+\sqrt p\,v_+.
$$

If the endpoints coincide, use any unit vector in $F$. In either case
$\langle v,Mv\rangle=t/B$, and the compressed identity

$$M_F-\widehat\zeta N_F=\widehat\rho I_F$$

gives $B\langle v,Nv\rangle=(t-B\widehat\rho)/\widehat\zeta$.
Split $\sqrt Bv=(x,y)$ and recover
$a=(x+y)/\sqrt2$, $b=(x-y)/\sqrt2$. These forces have exactly the target,
budget, and minimum absolute slope. The coefficients in $v$ are coherent
amplitudes; cross terms in both relevant compressed quadratic forms
vanish in the chosen eigenbasis.

### The nominal endpoint

At $B=t/\lambda$, every admissible normalized vector belongs to

$$G=\ker(M-\lambda I).$$

Let $\eta_-,\eta_+$ be the smallest and largest eigenvalues of
$N_G=P_GN|_G$. Then

$$
s_V(t/\lambda;t)
=\frac{t}{\lambda}\,\operatorname{dist}\bigl(0,[\eta_-,\eta_+]\bigr). \tag{4}
$$

Choose the endpoint eigenvector nearest zero if the interval does not
contain zero. If it contains zero, coherently combine its endpoint
eigenvectors with weights making the expectation zero. Recover $a,b$ as
above. Formula (4) avoids assuming a finite nonzero optimal multiplier at
this boundary. When only the positive extreme of $K$ has magnitude
$\lambda$, for example, this slope is $B$ times the smallest eigenvalue
of $D$ compressed to that phase eigenspace.

### Zero slope and proportional matrices

When $r>0$ and $B\geq t/r$, the existing opposite-extreme construction
attains zero slope at cost $t/r$. The reciprocal rescaling of $a,b$
described above raises its cost to exactly $B$ if required.

If $K=cD$ with $c\ne0$, then $\Theta=cq=-c\chi$, so at every feasible
budget

$$s_V(B;t)=t/|c|.$$

Equation (1) gives exactly the same value by taking $\zeta=c$. This
includes every nominally feasible one-dimensional force space. Thus a
zero robust efficiency is not confused with an asymptotically vanishing
slope at larger budgets.

## A physical two-tone example where the old certificate is strict

Choose $T=2\pi$ and the orthonormal forces
$u_1(t)=e^{it}/\sqrt{2\pi}$ and
$u_2(t)=e^{2it}/\sqrt{2\pi}$. Every force in their complex span is nominally
closed. Direct integration gives

$$
K=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1/2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
D=\begin{pmatrix}2&1/2\\1/2&1/2\end{pmatrix}.
$$

These matrices do not commute. Their nominal efficiency is $\lambda=1$,
and their robust multiplier and efficiency are

$$\zeta_*=3/5,\qquad r=\sqrt{13}/10.$$

For target $t=1$, the nominal budget $B=1$ forces the top phase
eigenvector, so (4) gives $s_V(1;1)=2$. The earlier certificate using only
$\zeta_*$ gives $(10-\sqrt{13})/6<2$.

Strict improvement also holds at an interior budget. At $B=5/4$, let

$$
u=\frac1{\sqrt5}\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt3\\-\sqrt2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
a=b=\sqrt{B/2}\,u.
$$

This pair has $\Theta=1$ and $|\chi|=(7-\sqrt6)/4$. It is optimal:
for $\widehat\zeta=\sqrt6/(3\sqrt6+1)$, $u$ is the largest-eigenvalue
eigenvector of $K-\widehat\zeta D$, whose positive extreme determines
its norm. The pair saturates the bound in (1). Thus

$$
s_V(5/4;1)=\frac{7-\sqrt6}{4}
>\frac{5(8-\sqrt{13})}{24},
$$

where the right side is the previous single-multiplier certificate.
This is an angle-sensitivity example: its full force space has no imposed
first-moment constraint, so the slope alone is not its complete thermal
infidelity coefficient.

## Exact leading thermal error after displacement constraints

For an original nominally closed space $V_0$, use its first-moment kernel

$$V_1=\left\{f\in V_0:\int_0^Tt f(t)dt=0\right\}.$$

Assume the target is feasible in $V_1$ at budget $B$, and compute (1) from
$K_1,D_1$ on that space. The established
[thermal-channel expansion](../archive/consolidation-2026-10-09/THEOREM.md#3-exact-leading-average-infidelity-coefficient)
gives, for every fixed finite thermal occupation,

$$
\boxed{\quad
\min_{\substack{f_1,f_2\in V_1,\ \Theta=\Theta_0\\ E\leq B}}
\lim_{\delta\to0}\frac{1-F_{\mathrm{avg}}(\delta)}{\delta^2}
=\frac45s_{V_1}(B;t)^2.
\quad} \tag{5}
$$

The attaining forces from above also attain (5), since both displacement
derivatives vanish throughout $V_1$. This minimizes a leading coefficient
for fixed pulses; it does not interchange a finite-detuning optimization
with the small-error limit.

On an unrestricted $V_0$, the two displacement terms still contribute.
There $(4/5)s_{V_0}(B;t)^2$ is only a lower bound on the optimum full-gate
coefficient. Neither (1) nor (5) is a finite-detuning performance guarantee
or a statement about input power or peak-limited controls.

## Mathematical attribution and verification

The convexity and support-function arguments use established numerical-range
theory. They are not a new general convexity or duality theorem. This result
completes the attainable sensitivity–cost relation for the physical control
class used throughout the repository.

- **[S17]** Joel H. Shapiro, *Notes on the Numerical Range*, 5 May 2017,
  [author-hosted notes](https://www.joelshapiro.org/Pubvit/Downloads/NumRangeNotes/numrange_notes.pdf),
  especially Theorem 6.1 on the Toeplitz–Hausdorff theorem. The theorem is
  classical; this reference supplies an accessible proof.
- The [targeted symbolic checks](../checks/check_sensitivity_frontier.py)
  verify the noncommuting two-tone example and boundary constructions.
  Analytical attainment and exact duality are proved above.
